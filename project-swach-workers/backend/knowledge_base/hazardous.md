# ⚠️ Standard Operating Procedure: Hazardous & Chemical Waste Management

## 1. Overview
Hazardous waste includes medical supplies (syringes, bandages), electronic components, chemicals, household cleaning agents, and batteries. These items pose severe health risks to workers and citizens and require STRICT isolation.

## 2. Equipment & PPE Required
* Chemical-resistant thick nitrile gloves (Double gloving required)
* Safety goggles
* N95 or particulate respirator mask
* Tongs/grabbers for sharp object collection
* Puncture-proof hazardous waste bags (Yellow color-coded)

## 3. Operations & Safety Protocol
1. **NO DIRECT CONTACT**: Do NOT touch medical waste, batteries, or liquid chemicals with bare hands. Use tongs or grabs.
2. **Containment**: Place sharp objects (needles, glass) into rigid containers first, then bag them. Place chemical containers and battery items into thick yellow bags.
3. **Discharge check**: Ensure there are no active liquid leaks. If a chemical spill is present, apply sand or absorbents before sweep.
4. **Isolation**: Keep hazardous waste completely isolated from general dry/wet waste inside the transport vehicle. Do not mix.

## 4. Disposal Target
* **HSR Specialized Bio-Medical Disposal Station (Sector 2)**.
* E-waste and battery items must go to the **Municipal Hazardous Recycling Yard (Sector 7)**.

## 5. Escalation & Priority
* Hazardous/Medical waste reports: **IMMEDIATE CRITICAL PRIORITY** (Must be accepted and cleared within 4 hours).
* Dispatcher must assign a worker equipped with a covered utility truck.
